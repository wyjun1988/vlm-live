# S1 checkpoint forensics - does real differ from control more than seed noise?

Materials: `outputs/server_0929/ckpt/{s1_real,s1_control,s1_control_seed1,s1_real_2b,s1_control_2b}/final.pt`
(+ `s1_real_seed1/final.pt`, truncated, partly salvaged). CPU only, no LLM loaded. Every number below was produced
by a script in this directory; the command is given with each block. All tables are regenerated by
`python outputs/server_0929/analysis/make_tables.py` from `forensics.json` and `function_level.json`.

## Short answer

- **Directly: no.** On every distance, same-seed `|real - control|` is *smaller* than `|control - control_seed1|`
  (e.g. function distance on identical CUT3R tokens 0.86-0.90 vs 1.03-1.05, T10). But that comparison is rigged in
  favour of "small": the same-seed pair shares the random init *and* the data order, the cross-seed pair shares
  neither (verified below: real and control of one seed start from bit-identical init).
- **The fair test is the 2x2 (condition x seed).** The real-minus-control difference is ~95% seed-specific: per-token
  cosine between (real - control) at seed 0 and at seed 1 is 0.04-0.07; the constant part of that difference has
  cosine 0.04 / 0.01 / 0.04 across seeds; the condition main effect exceeds the interaction by only 7-14% of its
  energy = 2.6-5.1% of the projector's output energy (T11). The constant "soft prompt" direction, which carries
  32-90% of the output energy (74% at L0, 83-90% at L0 with the pose embedding), is shared by all four 4B arms (cosine of the constant vectors 0.65-0.71 within a seed, 0.40-0.55 across seeds) with no real/control clustering.
- **There are weak but reproducible structural differences** (3/3 pairs: seed 0, seed 1, 2B): real's fc2 has a
  higher effective rank (L0 58/56/41 vs control 46/47/34, T7), real's output varies more between views
  (between-frame share 0.096/0.097/0.055 vs 0.062/0.065/0.044 at L0, T9), real's secondary fc2 output directions
  overlap more across seeds than control's (0.121 vs 0.067 at L0, T8), and the LayerNorm gain update's
  real - control direction repeats across seeds (cos 0.43 / 0.27 / 0.13, null 0.018, T6). These are consistent with a
  weak geometry-correlated gradient, but they cannot be separated from the donor mechanics of `--geom-control
  shuffled` (40% of donors have another grid shape and are bilinearly resampled; frames are cycled when image
  counts differ), and they are small in energy.
- **Why so little:** S1 was 106 optimizer steps (13,568 samples, 1.6% of an epoch) at lr 3e-5. Per element, AdamW
  moved the weights exactly as far as it does for zero-mean gradients (travel efficiency 0.13 vs 0.132 simulated,
  2B 0.20 vs 0.196): the size of every learned tensor is set by `lr x steps`, not by the task. The gates cannot move
  (max travel 1.6e-3; they end at 0.9993-0.9998).

## Method notes (read before the tables)

1. **Truncated seed-1 checkpoint - salvaged 24/28 tensors.** `torch.load` fails with
   `PytorchStreamReader failed reading zip archive: failed finding central directory`. The file has the full
   length (380,308,683 bytes) but its last 34,016,971 bytes are zeros (a hole: 676,352 allocated 512-byte blocks vs
   742,792 for the siblings; the upload tarball is 319,291,392 bytes and `tar tzvf` reports "Truncated input file").
   `salvage_seed1.py` walks the zip local headers from the front and checks each record's CRC-32 against its data
   descriptor: all 3 geometry projectors, all gates and the pose LayerNorm are intact (24 records CRC OK); the pose
   projector's `net.1.*` and `net.3.*` (record `data/24` onward) are lost. Output: `s1_real_seed1_salvaged.pt`, log
   `salvage_seed1.log`. Command: `python outputs/server_0929/analysis/salvage_seed1.py outputs/server_0929/ckpt/s1_real_seed1/final.pt`.
2. **Exact initial weights were rebuilt.** `train.py` calls `torch.manual_seed(seed + rank)` before building the
   model; the projector's fc1 is drawn with `kaiming_uniform_` = one mt19937 draw per element. `find_init_offset.py`
   regenerates the seed's uniform stream and finds where fc1 was drawn: offset **1,209,801,170** draws for every 4B
   arm (seed 0: real, control; seed 1: real_seed1, control_seed1) and **1,209,800,690** for both 2B arms (seed 0),
   24 consecutive elements matching within the Adam travel (`init_offsets.log`; the s1_real search printed
   `MATCH offset 1,209,801,170 max |du| 0.0088`). Rebuilding the modules after consuming that many draws gives an
   init under which **every** element of every tensor of every arm has |final - init| <= 0.73-0.84 x sum(lr)
   (`ckpt_forensics.log`); a wrong init would give ~22x. So: real and control of a seed start bit-identical; the
   2B pair too (no `--seed` passed = 0); seeds 0 and 1 have different random fc1 / pose `net.1`.
   Consequence: fc1, fc1.bias, fc2 columns and pose `net.1`/`net.3` columns are indexed by hidden units whose
   meaning is set by the seed's random init, so cross-seed cosines of those tensors are meaningless (marked `*`).
   Basis-free comparisons across seeds use fc2.bias, LayerNorm affine, output-side singular subspaces (T8), and
   the projector's function on shared inputs (T9-T11).
3. **Schedule.** AdamW(betas 0.9/0.98, wd 0), lr 3e-5, warmup 3, cosine to 0 over 106 steps (2B: 57 steps,
   warmup 1): sum(lr) = 1.62e-3 (2B 8.85e-4). Steps 100-105 carry 0.04% of the lr mass, so the weights at the
   last logged `inj` (step 100) equal final.pt for all practical purposes. Whether each arm ran all 106 steps
   cannot be read from the weights (the REPORT shows the last logged step 100/106); the `<arm>.log` would say.
4. **Travel efficiency calibration** (`adam_travel_sim.py`, log `adam_travel_sim.log`): AdamW with this schedule on
   per-element gradients `snr + N(0,1)` gives rms(update)/sum(lr) = 0.132 at snr 0, 0.141 at 0.05, 0.163 at 0.1,
   0.230 at 0.2, 0.711 at 1 (57 steps: 0.196 at snr 0).
5. **Function level** (`capture_tokens.py`, `function_level.py`): CUT3R (frozen, `cut3r_512_dpt_4_64.pth`, CPU)
   was run on the 16 uniform-oracle keyframes of 9 VSI videos (3 ARKitScenes, 3 ScanNet, 3 ScanNet++; image mode,
   keyframes only - what the VSI eval ingests with the default `--geom-stride`), tap layers 6/9/12 + pose token.
   Each arm's projectors were applied exactly as in `build_geometry_embeds` (merge 2, bilinear to the LLM grid
   from `smart_resize` with max_pixels 131072 -> 9x13 tokens), every 2nd token kept: 8,496 tokens per layer.
   Tokens are VSI keyframes, not Sensenova training images.

## Findings

### F1. real vs control is not separated from seed noise; the fair 2x2 says the difference is ~95% seed-specific

T11 (function level, identical inputs): per-token cosine of (real - control)@seed0 vs (real - control)@seed1 =
0.065 / 0.044 / 0.039 (L0/L1/L2); cosine of the constant parts of the two differences = 0.037 / 0.007 / 0.041.
Energy: condition 4.50 / 5.90 / 6.83 vs interaction 3.95 / 5.44 / 6.38 (ratio 1.14 / 1.09 / 1.07); the excess,
the only candidate for a reproducible real-vs-control function, is 5.1% / 2.9% / 2.6% of the mean per-arm output
energy (`anova_fractions.log`). The seed main effect is 1.6-1.9x the condition effect. In weight space the
basis-free vectors say the same: fc2.bias real - control cosines across seeds -0.045 / -0.017 / -0.005 (T6);
fc2.bias of all four 4B arms point the same way at cosine 0.47-0.53 regardless of condition (T4:
same-condition 0.470, 0.498 vs cross-condition 0.511, 0.488 at L0). Linear CKA is uninformative here: it is
0.78-0.998 for every pair, including cross-seed controls (T10).
Interpretation: what S1 learned in common across arms (the constant direction) is condition-independent; what
distinguishes real from control inside a seed does not come back with another seed. Caveat: only two seeds; the
excess energy has no error bar (it would need more seeds or a same-seed re-run to measure run-to-run divergence).

### F2. Direct |real - control| < |control - control_seed1|, but only because of shared init + order

Update distances (T3/T4, `forensics.json`): same-seed ndist of fc2.bias 0.84 vs cross-seed 1.03; LayerNorm bias
0.65 vs 1.36. Function distance (T10): real~control 0.902 / 0.856 / 0.870 (seed 0), 0.864 / 0.833 / 0.877 (seed 1),
0.675 / 0.693 / 0.720 (2B) vs control~control_s1 1.047 / 1.025 / 1.042 and real~real_s1 0.996 / 1.006 / 1.042.
Note what the same-seed number also says: two runs with the same init, same data order, same steps, differing
only in which geometry is fed, end with outputs whose per-token cosine is only 0.58-0.63 (4B) - the S1 end
point is extremely path-sensitive. Whether plain run-to-run GPU nondeterminism alone produces a similar spread
cannot be determined here (needs a same-seed re-run of one arm).

### F3. The learned tensors are sized by the optimizer budget, not by the task; gates are frozen

Per-element travel efficiency (T3): fc2 0.131-0.139 (4B) and 0.197-0.202 (2B) = the AdamW zero-SNR values 0.132 /
0.196 (method note 4); fc2.bias 0.123-0.134; fc1 / fc1.bias 0.17-0.25; LayerNorm 0.17-0.23 (4B). The noise-floor prediction
0.132 x sum(lr) x sqrt(numel) gives ||fc2||_F = 0.774 (4B) / 0.502 (2B); observed 0.768-0.817 / 0.506-0.517; pose
net.3 predicted 0.547, observed 0.540-0.550. Relative change from init of the random-init tensors: fc1 2.7-3.2%
(2B 1.8-2.3%), fc1.bias 2.6-3.9%, LayerNorm gain 0.03%, pose net.1 1.9-2.1% - the hidden features are the random
init. Gates (T2): 0.999349-0.999837, i.e. moved 1.6e-4 to 6.5e-4 from 1.0 (max possible 1.62e-3): the "learned
gate" is not a knob at this lr; all scale lives in fc2.
Interpretation: per element the gradient SNR is below ~0.05 on average. This does not make the function noise
(F4): Adam's per-element normalisation fixes the magnitude, the weak drift fixes the sign pattern.

### F4. The function is coherent and mostly a constant soft prompt (both arms alike)

`const_baseline.py` (`const_baseline.log`): on the same hidden features, the learned fc2 gives output rms 3.2-4.5,
a random Gaussian fc2 of the **same Frobenius norm** gives 0.17-0.23 (15-25x smaller), the learned fc2 with its
hidden columns permuted gives 0.25-0.99. So the output is dominated by a component coherently aligned with the
hidden features, not by weight noise. T9: the constant vector (identical for every token of every video) carries
74% / 68% / 48% of the output energy at L0 / L1 / L2 (real; control 74% / 65% / 49%), and 83-90% at L0 once the pose
embedding is added (pose alone: mean norm 2.8-3.2, 76-82% constant). The hidden features themselves are only
25-34% constant, and a random fc2 keeps that share (0.25-0.35), so the soft-prompt dominance is learned - the
gradient's most consistent request is "shift every visual token by one vector". The biases play no part in it:
||fc2.bias|| = 0.010 vs ||constant output|| 2.7-3.3. fc2's top singular direction is that constant (T7:
cos(u1, fc2.bias) 0.82-0.97; top-1 carries 25-42% of fc2 energy, top-16 67-94%).
Between-frame (view/scene-dependent) share: 2.5-12%; within-frame (spatial): 3-61%.

### F5. Reproducible real/control structural differences exist but are small and confounded

Across all three same-init pairs (seed 0, seed 1, 2B) and all three layers (9/9):
- fc2 effective rank real > control: L0 58.3 / 55.6 / 41.4 vs 46.1 / 46.5 / 33.7; L1 32.8 / 31.4 / 26.3 vs
  23.1 / 23.4 / 20.8; L2 11.0 / 10.8 / 10.4 vs 8.8 / 8.8 / 8.5 (T7). Seed spread within a condition (58.3 vs 55.6;
  46.1 vs 46.5) is far below the gap.
- between-frame share of the output real > control: L0 0.096 / 0.097 / 0.055 vs 0.062 / 0.065 / 0.044, L1
  0.054 / 0.063 / 0.044 vs 0.025 / 0.025 / 0.033, L2 0.049 / 0.065 / 0.056 vs 0.030 / 0.030 / 0.042 (T9); per-frame
  norm CV 0.13-0.15 vs 0.06-0.10 (4B).
- fc2 secondary output directions (2-17) overlap across seeds more for real (0.121 / 0.102 / 0.070) than for
  control (0.067 / 0.062 / 0.051) or across conditions (0.066-0.080 / 0.061-0.065 / 0.051-0.054); null 0.006 (T8).
- LayerNorm gain update: real - control repeats across seeds (cos 0.428 / 0.268 / 0.134 for the three projectors,
  pose LN 0.129; null 0.018-0.036), while LayerNorm bias and fc2.bias do not (T6).
- at L1/L2 the control projector outputs are larger on identical tokens (real/control 0.943 / 0.861 seed 0,
  0.942 / 0.832 seed 1; 2B L2 0.929) (T9, `anova_fractions.log`).
Interpretation: real geometry gives the projector a gradient that is a bit more input-dependent (more view-dependent,
higher-rank, reproducible secondary directions) - what one expects if some content signal exists. But (a) the
energy involved is a few percent (F1), and (b) the control is not "same inputs, other content": 40% of donors have
another grid shape and are bilinearly resampled and frames are cycled when image counts differ (REPORT: "same-grid
donors 60%"), which changes the input statistics - the LayerNorm-gain difference in particular is exactly the kind
of thing an input-statistics shift produces. These materials cannot separate the two.

### F6. The REPORT's end-of-S1 inj ratios are single-sample readings; the L0 gap is not in the weights

`inj` is logged from one micro-batch on rank 0 at step 100 (`measure` is set only at the last micro-step of a log
step). On identical tokens the L0 injection (projector + pose) of real and control is equal: rms 5.566 vs 5.558,
ratio 1.001 (seed 0). The REPORT has real/control 0.480/0.586 = 0.82 (seed 0) and 0.618/0.501 = 1.23 (seed 1) - the
sign flips between seeds. At L0 the denominator (layer-0 output of the frozen LLM for that sample's image) is the
same for both arms if they logged the same sample, which the shared seed and sampler imply; the gap then comes
from the numerator's input - real feeds the sample's own geometry, control a donor's - not from the weights.
Per-frame spread of the mean injection norm on VSI keyframes: CV 0.06-0.10 (L0+pose), 0.09-0.15 (L0 without pose)
(T9). At L1/L2 the weights do say control > real by 6% / 16-20% (F5), which the REPORT's single readings do not
reproduce (seed 0 L2 1.01). Scale: with inj ~0.5 at L0, the constant part alone (||const|| 5.06-5.13 of rms
5.56) shifts every visual token by roughly 0.4-0.55x the hidden-state norm (implied, not measured: the LLM was not run).
The 2B injections are ~0.7x the 4B ones (L0+pose rms 3.9-4.2) with 0.55x the lr mass.

### F7. Design redundancies found in the weights

- `pose_projector.net.3.bias` equals `projectors.0.fc2.bias` to <= 1.8e-8 in every arm (both get the same
  gradient: the pose embedding is added to the layer-0 embedding). Harmless here (bias norm 0.010), but the layer-0
  bias is effectively doubled and the pair is one parameter.
- The gate scalars are untrainable at lr 3e-5 over ~100 steps (F3); if they are meant as a learned injection
  strength they need their own lr group, otherwise they are dead weight.
- Zero-init fc2 on positive-mean GELU features makes a constant shift the cheapest thing to learn (F4); a
  per-frame mean-subtraction (or LayerNorm) after fc1, or penalising the constant component, would force the path
  to carry content if that is the intent. (Design suggestion, untested.)

### F8. What the weights imply for S2 (S2 checkpoints are not available - projections, not measurements)

- S2 (`--stage sft`) keeps the projector in the `--lr` group (3e-5) with LoRA at 1e-4; 136 steps give sum(lr) =
  2.07e-3, so S2 can move each projector element at most ~1.3x as far as S1 did. If its per-element gradients are as
  weak as S1's, the S2 projector is again budget-sized, and S2 starts from an injection that is 83-90% constant at
  L0 (F4) and whose real/control difference is mostly seed-specific (F1).
- So `s2_real - s2_control` at VSI will mostly measure what S2's LoRA does with two different-but-equivalent soft
  prompts plus a few percent of reproducible input dependence. `s2_real - s2_sft = -1.6 [-3.1, -0.1]` (REPORT) is
  consistent with the geometry path acting as a content-poor perturbation, but that reading needs `s2_control`.
- `s2_joint` (no S1) starts fc2 at zero and gets 2.07e-3 of projector lr mass; `s2_real` gets S1's 1.62e-3 on top
  (44% of its total). Given F1/F4, what S1 hands over is mainly a pre-grown constant shift; whether that matters
  is what `s2_joint - s2_real` measures (s2_joint VSI failed; unverified).
- The S2 inj readings in the REPORT (real 0.554/0.222/0.193 vs control 0.334/0.189/0.240; seed 1 0.493/0.224/0.168 vs
  0.402/0.173/0.169) are the same single-sample kind as S1's and cannot be read as weight differences (F6).
- For the S1 real VSI run (52.9, `format_hint: False` in `vsi/s1_real.json`, 0.0% format failures): the weights
  say the S1 injection is mostly a constant shift, which fits the M2 conclusion that S1 teaches format; without an
  `s1_control` VSI run (planned by the orchestrator, not in the upload) this cannot be attributed to content or not.

## Not determinable from these materials, and what would settle it

- Whether the real-vs-control excess (F1: 2.6-5.1% of output energy) is real: more seeds per condition, or a
  same-seed re-run of one arm to measure run-to-run divergence. The holdout-loss ablation of the S1 weights
  (`eval_geometry_ablation.py` real vs control, and control vs control_seed1 - running locally at the time of
  writing, output `ablation_s1_4b*.json`) settles it at the loss level. The server's `p5_ablation_s1.json` was never run.
- The training path: `<arm>.log` (loss, inj, gate every 20 steps), `probe.jsonl` (holdout real / shuffled / none
  every 500 steps - never reached at 106 steps), `train_args.json` of each arm (to confirm seed and lr).
- Whether the structural differences (F5) are geometry content or donor mechanics: a control whose donors are
  restricted to the same grid shape and image count (or a control fed real geometry of the same video at a
  different time) would remove the resampling/cycling confound.
- S2: `s2_*/final.pt`, `vsi/s2_control.json`, `p7_ablation_s2.json`.
- real_seed1's pose projector (lost in the upload): re-upload `s1_real_seed1/final.pt` (sha256 against the server).
